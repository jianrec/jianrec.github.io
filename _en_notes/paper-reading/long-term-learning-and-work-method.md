Core idea: invest a small amount of time consistently over the long run, study important fields systematically, keep producing meaningful work, and choose environments that foster personal growth.

## 1. Build a Literature Base

- Start by collecting papers, blog posts, internet resources and GitHub projects around a topic.
- Do a first pass to shortlist about 5 of them, discarding anything without substance.
- Identify the single most valuable one, use its references to find a sixth, and cross-reference to fill in your understanding.
- Reading 10–15 papers gives you a basic grasp of the field; reading 50–100 gives you a fairly deep understanding.

## 2. Read Papers in Multiple Passes

1. First pass: look at the title, abstract and figures to judge whether the paper is worth your time.
2. Second pass: focus on the introduction and conclusion, study the figures carefully, then read the main text.
3. Third pass: read the paper straight through, skipping complicated mathematics if needed.
4. Fourth pass: read it in full, initially skipping the parts you do not yet understand.
5. Prioritize the sections with the highest information density.

After reading, you should be able to answer:

- What problem are the authors trying to solve, and is that problem important and new?
- What are the core contributions and the key methods?
- Why does the method work, and how are the experiments designed?
- What are the limitations and underlying assumptions?
- What insights does it give you, and what can you reuse?
- Who are the key people in the field, and where is it heading?

## 3. Strengthen Long-Term Memory

- After reading, try to restate and memorize the core points.
- Review repeatedly through spaced learning to improve long-term retention.

## 4. Do Important Work

- Focus on skill growth and meaningful work rather than chasing job titles or company prestige.
- Weekends can go into an ongoing stream of papers, research, open source and courses.
- Reading two papers a week adds up to roughly 50 papers a year.
- What matters is not a short burst but sustained weekly investment; keeping it up for six months to a year produces visible accumulation.
- Let health, work and life blend together in a sustainable way.

## 5. Choosing a Job and a Role

- Choose a team where you can keep learning, work alongside excellent people, and take part in interesting, meaningful projects.
- Focus on teams of 10–30 people and their actual projects rather than simply chasing big-company brands.
- Before joining, get clear on: what projects you will actually work on, who you create value for, and how the team members will shape your growth.
- Most important of all is choosing work that maximizes your own potential, helps others, and keeps producing value.
